EXECUTIVE SUMMARY
Hospital appointment management in Pakistan continues to rely heavily on manual, telephone-based processes handled by reception staff during fixed working hours. These methods impose significant administrative burdens on hospital personnel, restrict patient access outside business hours, and frequently result in scheduling errors, miscommunications, and missed appointments. The absence of automated systems leads to double-bookings, high no-show rates, and fragmented record-keeping that prevents real-time visibility into appointment availability.
HALO (Hospital AI Liaison & Operations)
 is an agentic conversational appointment management platform designed to address these challenges for hospitals operating in Pakistan. The platform automates routine appointment-related interactions through conversational artificial intelligence capable of handling bookings, rescheduling, and cancellations without human intervention. Patients can interact with the HALO AI agent through a web-based text or voice interface embedded in hospital websites, or by dialing a hospital-provided phone number that connects directly to the AI agent. The system supports bilingual communication in English and Urdu to accommodate Pakistan's linguistic diversity.
HALO
 operates as a multi-tenant Software-as-a-Service (SaaS) platform, allowing multiple hospitals to onboard and maintain isolated data environments under a shared infrastructure. Hospital managers are provided with role-based dashboards to monitor appointments, manage doctor schedules, and record walk-in patients. A centralized administrative layer oversees hospital onboarding, subscription management, and platform governance. By reducing administrative workload, improving appointment accessibility, and minimizing scheduling conflicts, 
HALO
 establishes a scalable and efficient digital foundation for appointment management in the Pakistani healthcare system.
Abstract
The healthcare sector in Pakistan faces persistent operational inefficiencies in managing patient appointments due to heavy reliance on manual telephone-based or form-based scheduling systems. These traditional practices place significant administrative burden on hospital staff, restrict patient access to services outside working hours, and frequently result in scheduling errors and communication delays. To address these challenges, this project proposes 
HALO
, an agentic conversational appointment management platform designed specifically for hospitals operating in Pakistan. 
HALO
 will automate routine appointment-related interactions through conversational artificial intelligence capable of handling bookings, rescheduling, and cancellations without human intervention. The system will provide patients with voice-based and text-based interaction modes accessible through hospital website integrations, as well as a dedicated telephony-based interaction channel that allows patients to connect directly to the 
HALO
 AI agent via a hospital-provided phone number and will support bilingual communication such as English and Urdu. Real-time verification of doctoral availability and automated slot allocation will ensure accurate scheduling while minimizing conflicts and double bookings. The platform will include role-based management tools allowing hospital managers to monitor appointments, manage doctor schedules, and record in-person walk-in appointments when required. A centralized administrative layer will oversee hospital onboarding, subscription management, platform configuration. Unlike marketplace-based healthcare platforms, 
HALO
 will operate as a white-label solution, enabling individual hospitals to maintain institutional identity while benefiting from shared infrastructure. By reducing administrative workload, improving appointment accessibility, and minimizing scheduling errors, 
HALO
 aims to establish a scalable and efficient digital foundation for appointment management in the Pakistani healthcare system.

HALO (Hospital AI Liaison & Operations

The system is designed as a multi-tenant SaaS solution, enabling multiple hospitals to operate independently on a shared infrastructure while maintaining complete data isolation. Each participating hospital receives a dedicated AI-powered interface embeddable on its existing website, along with a hospital-provided phone number that connects directly to the HALO AI agent through telephony infrastructure. Problem Statement
Pakistani hospitals face significant operational challenges in managing patient appointments due to continued reliance on manual, telephone-based or form-based scheduling systems. Appointment requests are typically handled by reception staff during working hours only, leading to call congestion, unanswered inquiries, and extended waiting times when multiple patients attempt to book appointments simultaneously. These inefficiencies reduce patient satisfaction and delay access to healthcare services. Receptionists often balance in-person responsibilities alongside telephone scheduling, increasing the likelihood of missed calls, incomplete information exchange, and data entry errors. The absence of automated scheduling tools results in frequent problems such as double-bookings and miscommunication regarding doctor availability, particularly when schedule changes occur unexpectedly. Most hospitals lack after-hours appointment booking facilities, forcing patients to delay care or make unnecessary physical visits for scheduling purposes. Additionally, manual systems do not provide automated reminders, contributing to high appointment no-show rates that disrupt physician schedules and waste hospital resources. The use of fragmented record-keeping methods, including paper registers and disconnected spreadsheets, prevents accurate real-time visibility into appointment availability. Language diversity further complicates appointment management when communication occurs exclusively in English or Urdu, limiting effective access for certain patient groups. Collectively, these challenges increase administrative costs, reduce operational efficiency, and hinder optimal utilization of healthcare resources. An automated, bilingual, and continuously available appointment management system is required to address these limitations and improve healthcare service delivery in Pakistan.
Problem Solution
The 
HALO
 platform is designed to address the limitations of manual appointment management by introducing an agentic conversational system that automates routine scheduling tasks while supporting hospital-specific operational workflows. Each participating hospital will be provided with a dedicated interface integrated into its existing website, enabling deployment without extensive technical modifications.
HALO will employ conversational artificial intelligence to guide patients through appointment booking processes, including doctor selection by specialty or name, real-time availability checking, slot recommendation, and booking confirmation. The system will support voice-based interaction using speech recognition and synthesis, text-based interaction through a chatbot interface, and telephony-based voice interaction through a hospital-provided phone number connected directly to the 
HALO AI agent
, with bilingual support. Automated validation mechanisms will ensure that appointment slots are allocated accurately based on up-to-date doctor schedules, preventing double-bookings and scheduling conflicts. Confirmation and reminder notifications will be issued through supported communication channels to reduce missed appointments. Routine appointment handling will be fully automated, while hospital staff will retain the ability to record in-person walk-in appointments through management dashboards when required. Hospital managers will access role-based dashboards providing visibility into daily schedules, appointment status, and doctor availability. A centralized administrative module will manage hospital onboarding, verification, and subscription plans, ensuring consistent system governance. HALO will function as a multi-tenant software-as-a-service platform, allowing multiple hospitals to operate independently while sharing core infrastructure. By automating repetitive administrative processes and extending appointment services beyond conventional working hours, the system will improve operational efficiency, reduce administrative workload, and enhance patient access to healthcare services.

Scope
The scope of the HALO project covers the design and development of a web-based, artificial intelligence assisted appointment scheduling platform for hospitals operating in Pakistan. 
The system enables
 hospitals to register, undergo administrative verification, and create a dedicated instance to efficiently manage appointments. It provides a bilingual interface in English and Urdu and operates continuously, including outside regular working hours. 
Hospital Managers
 can configure hospital departments and doctor profiles, set consultation schedules and operational rules, access dashboards to monitor appointments, and manually add walk-in patients. 
Patients
 can access hospital-specific links embedded on hospital websites, interact with a conversational agent via text or voice through a web-based interface, 
or 
initiate interaction by dialing a hospital-provided phone number that connects directly to the HALO AI agent, and book, reschedule, or cancel non-emergency appointments based on real-time doctor availability. 
Platform Admins 
manage hospital approvals, system configurations, and platform governance. The system also includes features such as an automated appointment scheduling engine to prevent double bookings,  notifications and reminders via email and simulated messaging channels, and a multi-tenant architecture ensuring logical data isolation for each hospital. 
HALO
 
will not
 perform medical diagnosis, emergency handling, or clinical decision-making, and integration with external hospital management systems is excluded in the current scope.
Limitations/Constraints
The HALO platform is designed to automate administrative appointment management processes; however, the system operates under certain limitations and constraints that define its functional boundaries.
The artificial intelligence agent will not perform medical symptom analysis, disease diagnosis, or clinical decision-making, as the system is limited strictly to administrative appointment scheduling tasks.
The system will not handle medical emergencies, urgent care requests, or critical patient situations that require immediate medical intervention or emergency response coordination.
The system will support English and Urdu languages exclusively during the initial phase and will not accommodate additional regional or international languages.
Appointment categorization and specialist selection are based on patient-provided information; the system cannot validate medical necessity or assess the clinical appropriateness of requested appointments.
The platform will not include native mobile applications for Android or iOS devices and will be accessible through web browsers on desktop and mobile devices.

Literature Review
The development of HALO draws upon academic and industry research spanning conversational AI, healthcare informatics, natural language processing, and SaaS architecture.
Research by Laranjo et al. ("Conversational Agents in Healthcare," 
npj Digital Medicine
, 2018) established that AI-based conversational agents can effectively reduce administrative workloads in clinical settings while improving patient engagement. This validates HALO's core approach of deploying an autonomous conversational agent for appointment scheduling.
Ahmed et al. ("Conversational AI in Healthcare: Applications and Future Directions," 
ICAIM
, 2024) demonstrate that integrating NLU pipelines with healthcare scheduling systems can significantly improve slot utilization rates and reduce no-show incidents when combined with automated reminders.
Khan and Rahman ("Digital Transformation in Pakistani Healthcare: Challenges and Opportunities," 
Journal of Medical Systems
, 2023) specifically contextualize the digital health gap in Pakistan, noting that most facilities operate with minimal automation and that bilingual AI solutions present a viable path toward democratizing healthcare access.
The WHO Digital Health Implementation Framework (2023) provides guidelines relevant to multi-tenant healthcare platform governance that informed HALO's administrative module design, particularly around data isolation, hospital verification, and role-based access control.
Radford et al. (OpenAI, "Whisper: Robust Speech Recognition via Large-Scale Weak Supervision," 2022) validate the use of Whisper AI as a reliable multilingual speech recognition engine, supporting HALO's Urdu-language voice interaction capability.

Project Contribution
The HALO platform moves beyond standard digitization to provide an AI-assisted, deterministic appointment system. Key technical and conceptual contributions include:
Unified Multimodal AI:
 Introduces a synchronized interface where voice and text operate simultaneously, allowing users to switch modes seamlessly without losing context.
Localized Bilingual NLU:
 Implements a specialized Natural Language Understanding model optimized for Urdu and English, specifically handling code-switching common in Pakistan.
Autonomous Slot Negotiation:
 Unlike static forms, the AI agent actively negotiates alternative times when requested slots are unavailable, mimicking a human receptionist.
Zero-Friction "White-Label" Injection:
 utilizing a novel embeddable widget architecture that injects complex AI functionality into legacy hospital websites without backend restructuring.

User Classes and C
haracteristics
HALO is designed to serve three distinct user classes, each with clearly defined roles, responsibilities, access levels, and interaction patterns with the platform. The table below summarizes their characteristics.
Table 1: User Classes and Characteristics

Administrator
The Administrator is the owner and sole operator of the HALO SaaS platform. This user class is responsible for reviewing and approving hospital registration applications, rejecting invalid submissions, deactivating or suspending hospital accounts, configuring system-wide platform parameters, and monitoring aggregated platform-level statistics. The Administrator does not interact with any hospital\'s patient-facing appointment operations and has no access to patient data. The Hospital Manager is a verified staff member aka end user representing a registered hospital tenant on the HALO platform. The Patient is also as an end user who interacts with the HALO conversational AI agent to book, reschedule, or cancel non-emergency appointments at a registered hospital. Patients access the system through a hospital-specific web-embedded chat interface supporting text and real-time voice communication, or via a hospital-provided phone number connected to the HALO telephony channel. Patients do not require a registered account and interact through guided conversational flows conducted in English or Urdu. This user class has the highest interaction frequency across the platform. Patients do not access any administrative or management dashboards. Low to moderate technical proficiency is assumed. The system is designed to accommodate users with limited digital literacy.
Operating 
E
nvironment
The following operating environment requirements define the platforms, hardware, and network conditions under which HALO must operate correctly and consistently.
HALO is a web-based platform. It must operate correctly on modern desktop and laptop environments running Microsoft Windows 10/11, macOS 12 and above, and Ubuntu 20.04 LTS and above.
The system must function seamlessly across the following web browsers: Google Chrome (v110+), Mozilla Firefox (v110+), Microsoft Edge (v110+), and Apple Safari (v16+). No browser-specific plugin or extension shall be required.
The Hospital Manager and Administrator interfaces must be fully accessible and usable on tablet devices (minimum 768px viewport width) through a responsive web layout. Mobile browser access is supported for read-only views.
The Patient-facing HALO conversational widget is embedded into hospital websites and must load correctly on any device with a modern browser, including smartphones, supporting screen widths from 320px and above.
The backend API server runs on Node.js (v20.2 LTS) with Express.js (v4.3). The AI service layer runs on FastAPI (v0.104) with Python (v3.11). Both are deployed in Docker containers (v24.1) and hosted on a cloud infrastructure provider.
The primary database is PostgreSQL (v15+). Redis (latest) is used for session caching. Both database services are hosted on the same cloud environment as the backend.
The telephony interface requires the hospital to possess a configured SIP/VoIP number. The HALO AI agent connects to this number via Twilio or an equivalent telephony API. Patients dial this number directly from any standard telephone or mobile phone.
The bilingual NLU pipeline (English and Urdu) requires the Whisper AI (Large-v3) model for speech recognition and the Vicuna (13B-v1.5) model via Ollama for conversational generation. These AI services run on the FastAPI microservice layer.
The system requires a stable internet connection for all AI-driven appointment interactions and administrative operations. A minimum upload/download speed of 5 Mbps is recommended for the voice interaction mode.
 
HALO operates as a multi-tenant SaaS platform. Each registered hospital tenant is logically isolated within the shared infrastructure. No cross-tenant data access is permitted at any la
yer.
Design and Implementation Constraints
The development of HALO is subject to the following technical and operational constraints that restrict the options available to developers and define the non-negotiable boundaries of implementation.
The frontend must be developed using Next.js (v14.6) with Tailwind CSS (v3.3) and TypeScript (v5.2). No alternative frontend framework is permitted.
The backend API must be built using Node.js (v20.2 LTS) with Express.js (v4.3) following RESTful JSON conventions. The AI microservice must use FastAPI (v0.104) with Python (v3.11).
All data storage must be used in PostgreSQL exclusively. MongoDB or any NoSQL solution is not permitted for primary data storage.
The conversational AI pipeline must use LangChain (v0.1.4) as the orchestration framework. The NLU layer must implement intent classification and named entity recognition. Dialogue state management must use a Finite State Machine (FSM) to ensure deterministic and auditable conversation flows.
The speech recognition component for Urdu and English voice input must use Whisper AI (Large-v3). Text-to-Speech output must use a WebRTC-compatible synthesis engine.
Authentication must use JWT (stateless tokens) with SSL/TLS encryption on all data in transit. No plain HTTP communication is permitted.
The platform must enforce role-based access control (RBAC) at the API level. Endpoints accessible to a Hospital Manager must not be reachable by a Patient, and vice versa.
HALO supports English and Urdu exclusively. No additional language is in scope for this version.
No native mobile application (Android or iOS) is in scope. The system is accessible through web browsers on all device types.
The platform must not perform medical diagnosis, clinical decision-making, or emergency handling. The AI agent is strictly limited to administrative appointment scheduling tasks.
Multi-tenancy requires strict logical data isolation per hospital at the database schema level using tenant identifiers. Shared infrastructure is used but no tenant may access another tenant's data.
Billing and subscription simulation is used for academic evaluation. No live payment gateway integration is required in this release.
Patient data collected shall be limited to name, contact number, email, age, and visit reason 
while
 no clinical or medical data shall be stored
.

Non-Functional Requirements
This section specifies non-functional requirements (NFRs) other than constraints and external interface requirements. Non-functional requirements define the quality attributes of the 
HALO
 system, such as reliability, usability, performance, and security.
Requirements
.
Reliability
 
The HALO platform shall achieve a minimum uptime of 99% measured on a monthly basis, excluding scheduled maintenance windows communicated at least 
24 hours
 in advance.
The AI conversational agent shall gracefully handle unrecognized inputs or pipeline failures by returning a fallback response to the patient rather than terminating the conversation session.
Appointment booking transactions shall be 
ACID-compliant
, ensuring that partial failures during slot allocation do not result in inconsistent database states such as double-bookings.
In the event of a backend service failure, the system shall maintain patient session state for up to 
5 minutes
 to allow reconnection without losing conversation context.
Usability
The patient-facing conversational interface shall be operable by a first-time user without any prior training, requiring no more than 
3 conversational turns
 to initiate a booking request.
The system shall support voice interaction as a complete alternative to text interaction, ensuring that patients who cannot type can complete the full appointment booking flow through voice alone.
The Hospital Manager dashboard shall display all key daily appointment information above the fold without requiring scrolling on a 
standard 1080p
 desktop display.
Error messages and system prompts shall be presented in plain language in both English and Urdu, avoiding technical jargon.
Performance
The AI agent shall respond to patient inputs within 
3 seconds
 for text-based interactions under normal server load conditions.
Voice recognition output shall be available to the conversational pipeline within 2 seconds of the patient completing a spoken utterance.
The Hospital Manager dashboard shall load all daily appointment data within 
4 seconds
 on a stable broadband connection.
The appointment slot availability check shall complete within 
1 second
 to ensure a seamless booking conversation flow.
The system shall support at least 
100 concurrent
 patient conversations without degradation in response time.
Security
 
All user authentication shall use JWT tokens with a maximum token lifetime of 
8 hours
; tokens shall be invalidated on logout.
All data transmission between clients and the HALO server shall be encrypted using TLS 
1.2 or higher
; plain HTTP connections shall be rejected.
Patient data stored in the database shall be limited to: name, contact number, email address, age, and visit reason. No clinical or medical data shall be stored.
Role-based access control shall enforce that a Hospital Manager can only access data belonging to their own hospital tenant. Cross-tenant data access attempts shall be logged and rejected.
Passwords shall be stored using a 
strong cryptographic
 hashing algorithm. Plaintext passwords shall never be stored or logged.
External Interface Requirements
This section provides information to ensure that the 
HALO
 system will communicate properly with users and with external hardware or software elements.
User Interfaces Requirements
The patient-facing conversational interface shall follow a clean, minimal design with a chat window, voice activation button, and hospital branding elements.
Font sizes shall be a minimum of 
14px
 for body text to ensure readability on mobile screens.
The Hospital Manager dashboard shall use a sidebar navigation structure with clearly labeled sections: Dashboard, Doctors, Appointments, Walk-ins, Analytics, and Settings.
The Admin dashboard shall use a top-navigation layout with sections: Hospitals, Subscriptions, Platform Settings, and Reports.
Color-coded status indicators shall be used for appointment statuses: Green = Confirmed, Yellow = Pending, Red = Cancelled, Grey = Completed.
Software interfaces
PostgreSQL Database:
 The HALO backend API (Node.js/Express.js) shall maintain a persistent connection to a PostgreSQL database instance for all structured data operations including appointments, doctor schedules, and hospital profiles.
FastAPI AI Microservice:
 The Node.js API shall communicate with the Python/FastAPI AI microservice via authenticated internal HTTP API calls to process NLU requests, generate AI responses, and perform speech recognition.
Whisper AI (Large-v3):
 The AI microservice shall interface with the locally hosted Whisper AI model for Urdu and English speech-to-text conversion during voice interactions.
LangChain + Vicuna (Ollama):
 The AI microservice shall use LangChain (version 0.1.4) to orchestrate conversational dialogue with the locally hosted Vicuna 13B model served via Ollama.
Email Service:
 The backend shall interface with an SMTP-compatible email service (e.g., SendGrid or SMTP relay) to dispatch appointment confirmations and reminders.
WebRTC:
 Real-time voice input/output (microphone capture and audio playback) for the browser-based voice interaction mode is handled through the WebRTC API.
Twilio Telephony API:
 The hospital-provided phone number channel is integrated via Twilio (or equivalent SIP/VoIP provider) to route incoming patient calls to the HALO AI voice agent.
Hardware interfaces
The patient-facing voice interaction feature requires access to the patient's device microphone through browser-standard Web Audio API permissions. No proprietary hardware is required.
The telephony-based interaction channel requires integration with a third-party telephony provider (e.g., 
Twilio
) to route incoming calls to the HALO AI agent. Hardware PSTN infrastructure is managed by the telephony provider.
Communications interfaces
Email confirmations and reminders shall be sent to the patient's provided email address and to the Hospital Manager's registered email. Emails shall include appointment details, reference ID, and a plain-text fallback for email clients that do not render HTML.
In-app notifications shall be delivered to Hospital Manager sessions in real time using WebSocket connections managed by the Node.js backend.
WebRTC
 shall be used for real-time audio streaming between the patient's browser and the HALO AI voice agent during web-based voice interactions.
Telephony audio shall be streamed to the HALO AI agent via the telephony provider's API (e.g., Twilio Media Streams) for speech recognition and response synthesis.
